The dataset

Field measurements, not a toy example

The UCI Air Quality dataset records a chemical multisensor device deployed in a significantly polluted area at road level in an Italian city. It pairs sensor responses with reference-analyzer concentrations and meteorological measurements.

Project question

Can simple baselines forecast daily NO₂?

We study `NO2(GT)`, the reference-measured nitrogen dioxide concentration in µg/m³. After parsing timestamps and treating the dataset's -200 missing-value marker correctly, the notebook compares three forecasts over the final 30 calendar days.

Forecasts: last observed value, training-period weekday average, and a linear time trend.

What the notebook does

01 · SOURCE

Load the original archive

Download the UCI ZIP, parse its semicolon CSV and comma decimals, and inspect the 15 sensor, pollutant, and weather fields.

02 · UNDERSTAND

Check gaps and patterns

Fix date and time formats, convert missing sentinels, chart hourly NO₂, and quantify target missingness before aggregation.

03 · FORECAST

Compare on future dates

Aggregate to daily means and score all baselines on the same held-out period using MAE, RMSE, and sMAPE.

Limitations

  • One sensor deployment in one Italian city.
  • Roughly one year of data, so annual seasonality is difficult to establish.
  • Missing readings can be systematic; the notebook does not fill target gaps to inflate coverage.
  • A single 30-day holdout is a baseline comparison, not a validated operational forecast.

Data use

UCI describes this dataset as intended for research and provides a CC BY 4.0 license in its metadata. Review the repository's current dataset terms and cite De Vito et al. (2008) when using the data.
